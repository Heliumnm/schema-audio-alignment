\begin{abstract}
Clinical audio models increasingly align recordings with participant metadata
or metadata-derived text, but these targets also encode demographics, symptoms,
missingness, and cohort structure. We introduce a Pairing-Controlled Transfer
Audit that trains matched trajectories under correct pairing, global shuffling,
disease-label-preserving shuffling, and a post-hoc
label-and-recorded-sex-preserving control, then freezes endpoints for profile
correspondence, attribute decodability, and disease prediction after covariate
balancing. In UKCOVID, Correct improves profile retrieval and sex decodability
over label-only shuffling for AST-6L and OPERA-CT. Preserving label and recorded
sex substantially attenuates the sex-probe contrast across AST-6L, OPERA-CT,
and HeAR while residual retrieval differences remain. Source-only matched
disease gains, sex-preserving comparisons, and target-assisted comparisons
remain uncertain. Secondary CODA TB, Cambridge COVID-19 Sounds, and Coswara
analyses extend the descriptive pattern without establishing a universal null.
Profile-correspondence gain and disease transfer are distinct empirical claims.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
clinical audio, audio--metadata alignment, contrastive learning,
representation auditing, distribution shift
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Clinical audio models increasingly use participant metadata not only as
prediction-time context, but also as supervision for representation learning.
Prior work has used metadata to define contrastive pairs, construct textual
supervision, or provide multimodal context for downstream diagnosis
\cite{soni2022contrastive,moummad2023metadata,kim2024bts,siam2026respiramfm}.
Recent audio-language systems further translate structured metadata into
clinical descriptions and align them with frozen or pretrained audio
representations \cite{ilerisoy2026zeroshot,ando2025slap}. This line of work is
appealing because clinical labels are scarce while patient profiles are often
available.

The same design creates an identification problem. A contrastive objective
specifies that paired modalities should be close, but does not specify which
shared attributes should support that correspondence. Clinical metadata is
heterogeneous: it can encode disease-related information, symptoms,
demographics, missingness patterns, and cohort structure. Thus an apparent
alignment gain can reflect profile correspondence, label-conditioned population
association, or disease information that transfers after the population changes.
COVID audio screening already illustrates how apparent performance can collapse
after symptom and demographic adjustment
\cite{coppock2024audio}, and contrastive objectives are known to admit shortcut
solutions when the discrimination task can be solved by non-target features
\cite{robinson2021shortcut}. More generally, explicit multimodal alignment can
help or hurt depending on what information is shared across modalities
\cite{fang2026align}.

The remaining identification problem is how the pairing relation should be
controlled when clinical metadata retains disease-label and demographic
structure. Related work has compared correct and shuffled audio-description
trajectories, and clinical vision-language work has used pathology-matched
candidates to distinguish instance-level linkage from broader diagnostic
similarity \cite{min2026semantic,arasteh2026linkage}. These controls motivate
our setting but do not resolve it: a global shuffle removes both the observed
participant pairing and the label-conditioned metadata association. We compare
Correct ($C$), Within-label ($W$), and Global ($G$) pairing. Within-label assigns
metadata from another participant with the same disease label, so $C-W$ asks
what the observed participant--profile pairing adds beyond label-conditioned
metadata association. $W-G$ measures the effect of retaining that label
restriction. A post-hoc control, \wys{}, additionally restricts donors to the
same recorded sex. Disease labels construct these control pairings; they are not
included in the metadata text.

Our Pairing-Controlled Transfer Audit follows this matched-trajectory logic.
Frozen audio and metadata encoders are shared across arms; the projector
initialization, audio order, optimizer schedule, and training duration are
matched; only the metadata donor assigned to each audio example changes. The
resulting endpoints are frozen and evaluated through three separate questions:
did the assigned pairing affect profile retrieval, what attributes became more
decodable, and did disease prediction improve after covariate balancing? This
separation is important because probe accuracy alone need not imply task use
\cite{ravichander2021probing}. Here, transfer means evaluation on a
covariate-balanced target \emph{within each dataset}, not deployment of one
model across datasets. The contribution is an audit of distinct empirical
claims, not a claim that metadata alignment is generally ineffective or that
the controls isolate causal information components.

\section{Data and Methods}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Datasets and evidence status}

UKCOVID is the discovery dataset \cite{budd2024ukcovid}. After objective audio
filtering, Standard train contains 20,714 participants. Evaluations use
Standard test ($n=11{,}119$), matched ($n=1{,}814$), and a participant-disjoint
matched-long set ($n=4{,}196$). Recruitment source alone has COVID AUROC
0.9966 in Standard train and 0.5000 after matching. This motivates testing
under the selected change in cohort association; it does not show that a
disease classifier causally uses recruitment source. UKCOVID test results
informed earlier protocol development, so this is an exploratory audit rather
than a locked confirmatory benchmark.

Secondary analyses use CODA TB (1,081 eligible participants)
\cite{huddart2024coda}, Cambridge COVID-19 Sounds (989)
\cite{xia2021covidsounds}, and Coswara (1,701)
\cite{bhattacharya2023coswara}, each with a 200-participant matched target.
CODA uses a fixed 100-pair target from released training data; Cambridge is a
strict-COVID reconstruction of its Task-2 cough subset, not an official
benchmark replication; Coswara uses model-blind secondary matching but is a
post-hoc stress test. These analyses have different evidence status and do
not constitute independent confirmations of a null disease effect.
Matching balances selected measured covariates, not all sources of bias.

\subsection{Frozen encoders and pairing-controlled trajectories}

We evaluate three frozen UKCOVID audio backbones spanning different pretraining
domains: AST-6L, derived from a general-audio AST; OPERA-CT, pretrained for
respiratory audio; and HeAR, pretrained for health acoustics. AST-6L retains
six blocks of the 12-layer AudioSet-finetuned AST \cite{gong2021ast}; the
depth was fixed before this audit from independent respiratory-audio
comparisons and not from UKCOVID matched performance. OPERA-CT is the released
768-dimensional respiratory contrastive Transformer \cite{zhang2024opera}.
Its pretraining includes UKCOVID training audio, making it a domain-pretrained
comparator. Participant-disjoint splits in this study do not by themselves
verify exclusion from upstream pretraining. AST-6L and OPERA-CT define the
original audit, whereas HeAR is reported as a post-hoc extension.

Backbone-specific preprocessing produces one aggregated audio representation
per participant. A frozen Phi-2 encoder \cite{javaheripi2023phi2} embeds a
deterministic schema of available demographics, smoking status, respiratory
history, symptoms, and explicit missing values. Disease labels, participant
identifiers, recruitment source, site, country, platform, device, timestamps,
and recording artefacts are excluded from this text.

Let $a_i,m_i,y_i,s_i$ denote audio, metadata, disease label, and recorded sex.
For arm $A$, $\pi_A(i)$ is the metadata donor assigned to audio participant $i$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 C &: \pi_C(i)=i,\\
 W &: \pi_W(i)\ne i,\quad y_{\pi_W(i)}=y_i,\\
 G &: \pi_G(i)\ne i,\\
 \wys{} &: \pi_{W_{y,s}}(i)\ne i,\quad
          (y_{\pi_{W_{y,s}}(i)},s_{\pi_{W_{y,s}}(i)})=(y_i,s_i).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pairing}
\end{equation}
Global imposes no label or sex restriction, although accidental matches can
remain. These four assignments define matched training trajectories: the audio
examples, optimizer, seeds, and training length are held fixed while
$\pi_A$ changes. We use \dpair{} for $C-W$ and \dlabel{} for $W-G$, evaluated
separately for each frozen endpoint. These contrasts test pairing constraints;
they are not causal estimands or a decomposition into disjoint information
components. $R$ denotes the corresponding frozen raw-audio representation.

The \wys{} control uses the same 20,714 training participants and rejects
self-pairs, singleton strata, and cross-stratum fallback. The five-participant
positive/missing-sex stratum supports valid reassignment. Different-person
pairs retain identical metadata text in 6.35--6.61\% of \wys{} pairs and
3.03--3.33\% of $W$ pairs. Thus, donor reassignment is not equivalent to
changing every semantic target.

\subsection{Projector training and frozen endpoint evaluation}

Only the audio-side projector is trained. Let
$t_i^A=e_{\mathrm{text}}(m_{\pi_A(i)})$ be its assigned frozen text target.
For a batch $\mathcal B$ of size $B$, the one-directional objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_A=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}
\log\frac{\exp(\bar f_\theta(a_i)^\top\bar t_i^A/\tau)}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}\exp(\bar f_\theta(a_i)^\top\bar t_j^A/\tau)},
\quad \tau=0.07,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where bars denote $\ell_2$ normalization. The projector is trained for five
seeds and 500 epochs, using the final checkpoint without epoch selection.
Within a seed, arms share initialization, audio batches, and dropout streams;
pairing assignments are fixed, and only the metadata donor changes. The final
checkpoint is then frozen for all downstream evaluations. This audits a
paper-faithful Stage-1 alignment implementation, not the full RespiraMFM
downstream Phi-2/LoRA system.

\textbf{Endpoint 1: correspondence.} Source-validation audio queries retrieve metadata
profiles. Identical schemas are one semantic target; macro-profile mean
reciprocal rank (MRR) prevents frequent profiles from dominating the reported
average. Separate post-hoc diagnostics restrict candidates by recorded sex,
or by disease label and recorded sex. These restrictions change the retrieval
task, not the trained representation; labels define diagnostic candidate sets
rather than encoder inputs.

\textbf{Endpoint 2: decodability.} Identically specified regularized probes predict
recorded sex, age, symptoms, recording/cohort variables, and disease. Probe
performance concerns the specified readout, not causal use by a disease
classifier.

\textbf{Endpoint 3: disease prediction.} Metrics are AUROC, negative log-likelihood
(NLL), and Brier score. For the primary source-only endpoint, source data
select and calibrate readouts before evaluation on matched targets.
Matched splits do not select the projector or source-only readout.
References include frozen raw audio and metadata only. A fixed multilayer
perceptron (MLP) tests one alternative to the linear readout. A separate
target-assisted diagnostic trains, selects, and calibrates the readout on
matched-long before evaluation on participant-disjoint matched participants.
A further diagnostic fits a one-dimensional calibrator on matched-long for
transport to matched. Neither diagnostic is a source-only result.

\subsection{Uncertainty and analysis scope}

Reported 95\% bootstrap intervals jointly resample profiles and seeds for
macro-profile retrieval, and participants and seeds for probes and disease
metrics, retaining arm and seed pairing. Participants, not matched-pair
blocks, are the reported disease resampling units. We interpret these
intervals descriptively for each comparison, not as simultaneous evidence
across all settings. The data-informed analysis sequence is exploratory;
pointwise intervals do not resolve uncertainty from analysis selection.
An interval including zero is not an equivalence result.

\subsection{Label-conditioned trajectories differ from global shuffling}

Within-label and Global differ even though neither preserves the original
donor identity. For UKCOVID AST, source $W-G$ probe AUROC differences are
$0.0646$ for COVID, $0.0758$ for cough, and $0.0647$ for recruitment source;
the reported intervals exclude zero. On matched participants, the COVID
difference is $0.0126$ with an interval including zero, while the cough
difference is $0.0803$. Thus, restricting metadata donors by label changes
readout-accessible information even without restoring the original participant
pairing. A $C-G$ comparison alone cannot distinguish this label-conditioned
association from the additional $C-W$ contrast.


\subsection{Correct trajectories alter correspondence and sex decodability}

Table~\ref{tab:contrasts} shows positive $C-W$ retrieval estimates across the
three UKCOVID backbones; all five seed-level estimates are positive for each
primary backbone. This is the manipulation check for the matched trajectories:
changing only the assigned metadata donor produced a detectable frozen-endpoint
difference. It does not imply complete learning of a clinical profile or
exclude other alignment limitations.

For sex decodability, the large $C-W$ contrasts become much smaller when $W$
is replaced by \wys{}, while $\wys{}-W$ remains positive. The sex-probe
contrast is therefore highly sensitive to whether the pairing control preserves
recorded-sex consistency. Because \wys{} also preserves correlated metadata
structure and more frequently retains an unchanged semantic profile than $W$,
we do not interpret this comparison as a sex-specific causal decomposition.
It also neither establishes equivalence between $C$ and \wys{} nor shows that
a disease classifier uses sex.
Raw audio generally has higher absolute demographic decodability than aligned
representations, so a positive $C-W$ contrast should not be read as an
improvement over raw audio. Age-probe increases were reported for AST but not
OPERA; age was not isolated by an additional pairing control.

Retrieval is different. Residual $C-\wys{}$ MRR estimates are positive for all
three backbones, with pointwise intervals above zero for AST and HeAR.
The AST interval is close to zero at its lower bound ($0.00001$).
Sex-probe attenuation therefore does not establish that sex consistency
explains all profile correspondence.

Under same-sex candidate restriction, $C-W$ MRR is
$0.00140$ [$-0.00065$, $0.00350$],
$0.00060$ [$-0.00143$, $0.00256$], and
$0.00356$ [$0.00096$, $0.00638$] for AST, OPERA, and HeAR.
Under same-label-and-sex restriction, it is
$0.00245$ [$-0.00001$, $0.00499$],
$0.00094$ [$-0.00149$, $0.00329$], and
$0.00451$ [$0.00115$, $0.00813$].
We interpret contrasts within each candidate rule. Different candidate
libraries change the rank distribution, so comparisons across rules do not
quantify a fraction of retrieval gain attributable to sex. The HeAR results
support residual correspondence under both restricted candidate rules.

\subsection{Frozen disease endpoints do not show a consistent transfer gain}

Table~\ref{tab:absolute} places the paired contrasts alongside absolute
AUROC. $C-W$ disease differences vary in sign across the primary backbones,
and Correct is not uniformly above raw audio. The added \wys{} comparison
also remains uncertain: $C-\wys{}$ is $-0.0003$ for AST, $0.0009$ for OPERA,
and $0.0130$ for HeAR, with all intervals including zero
(Table~\ref{tab:contrasts}). The HeAR interval [$-0.0023$, $0.0287$] still
allows a positive effect; it is not evidence of equivalence.
Likewise, $\wys{}-W$ disease differences are directionally mixed and
uncertain. The large change in sex-probe contrasts therefore does not
establish a corresponding disease-prediction advantage or disadvantage.

A fixed MLP gives $C-W$ disease AUROC differences of
$0.0050$ [$-0.0154$, $0.0270$] for AST and
$-0.0052$ [$-0.0208$, $0.0110$] for OPERA. This tests one nonlinear readout;
it does not rule out disease information accessible by other readout
conditions.
Target-assisted $C-W$ estimates are
$-0.0082$ [$-0.0337$, $0.0177$],
$0.0287$ [$-0.0017$, $0.0626$], and
$0.0047$ [$-0.0278$, $0.0408$] for AST, OPERA, and HeAR.
In particular, OPERA's interval allows a sizable benefit. No consistent
advantage over $W$ or $R$ was established under this diagnostic, but separate
intervals do not test an interaction between pairing and readout-training
regime.

Across the 12 dataset--backbone settings, all $C-W$ retrieval point estimates
are positive, with 11 reported intervals above zero. All 12 sex-probe
$C-W$ intervals are above zero. For the primary matched disease contrast
$C-\wys{}$, five point estimates are positive and seven are negative, with no
interval entirely above zero. Across the nine locked secondary settings, these
estimates range from $-0.0634$ to $0.0476$. These descriptive counts show a
recurring pattern in result shape: correspondence and sex-probe contrasts are
more consistently positive than matched disease contrasts in the audited
settings. They are not independent replications, pooled effects, or evidence
that all disease effects are zero. Diseases, candidate libraries, and analysis
histories differ, and the external targets remain small.


\subsection{Secondary fusion and probability-scale diagnostics}

On UKCOVID matched, adding raw audio to metadata changes AUROC by
$0.0028$ [$-0.0016$, $0.0073$] for AST and
$0.0041$ [$-0.0006$, $0.0087$] for OPERA.
Metadata plus Correct exceeds metadata plus Within-label by
$0.0156$ [$0.0034$, $0.0276$] for AST and
$0.0021$ [$-0.0068$, $0.0107$] for OPERA.
Neither comparison establishes a consistent advantage over metadata plus
raw audio. Different interval outcomes do not establish that the two
backbone effects differ.

For source-calibrated predictions, the $C-W$ differences in
$-\mathrm{NLL}$ are $-0.0249$ [$-0.0443$, $-0.0069$] for AST and
$-0.0176$ [$-0.0335$, $-0.0010$] for OPERA. Target calibration reduces these
gaps to approximately zero with unchanged AUROC. This is consistent with a
probability-scale adjustment, not evidence of new ranking information or a
unique explanation of the original discrepancy.


\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central result is not that alignment fails, but that a detectable
correspondence gain does not determine its clinical interpretation. $W-G$
demonstrates a difference associated with label-restricted pairing. Adding
recorded-sex consistency substantially reduces the sex-probe contrast with
Correct, yet this control is not a pure intervention on sex: it also preserves
correlated metadata structure and more frequently retains an unchanged semantic
profile. Residual profile-retrieval differences remain. These are distinct
observations, not a causal decomposition or a participant-identity result. The
audit therefore adds information beyond a Correct-versus-Global ablation alone.

Disease prediction must be evaluated separately. The reported disease
contrasts do not establish a consistent benefit across backbones, controls,
or readout regimes, but several intervals permit beneficial effects. Retrieval
gains are therefore insufficient as evidence of disease benefit in this
pipeline. Conversely, uncertain disease contrasts do not show that alignment is
generally ineffective or that aligned representations contain no usable disease
information. Matching also defines a particular target population rather than
isolating a purely pathological acoustic signal.

The evidence is exploratory. UKCOVID influenced protocol development; HeAR,
\wys{}, and target-assisted readouts are post-hoc; external targets have
limited precision. Pointwise bootstrap intervals do not account for the
full analysis-selection process or preserve matched-pair blocks. Upstream
pretraining exposure is not resolved by within-study participant separation.
The sex-preserving control does not isolate age, other attributes, or their
interactions, and may retain additional structure correlated with its
conditioning variables. The experiment audits one projector implementation,
not a complete multimodal system.

The synthetic study failed its preregistered general-mechanism gates, so we
do not claim a universal causal mechanism. A proposed disease-invariant
repair was not trained because the cohorts lacked the prespecified support
for cross-environment same-label positives and within-environment
opposite-label negatives; this is not a negative performance result for
that untrained method.

\section{Conclusion}

Conditioning the pairing control on disease label and recorded sex changes
the interpretation of a large sex-probe contrast without eliminating all
observed retrieval differences. Neither the original nor the expanded
pairing comparisons establish a consistent improvement in matched disease
prediction, and individual benefits remain compatible with the intervals.
Clinical audio--metadata alignment should therefore be assessed through
separate correspondence, decodability, and disease-prediction endpoints,
with raw-audio references and explicit uncertainty.
